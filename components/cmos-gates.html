Logic Gates from Transistors

A CMOS gate has two transistor networks. A pull-up network of PMOS transistors can connect the output to VDD (logic 1); a pull-down network of NMOS transistors can connect it to GND (logic 0). For every input combination exactly one of them conducts.

A PMOS conducts when its gate is 0, an NMOS when its gate is 1. Watch the gate beads switch the transistors first; then charge flows along the shaded path, filling the output node from VDD or draining it to GND.

Complementary networks

The pull-down network is the dual of the pull-up: transistors in series in one are in parallel in the other. NAND has PMOS in parallel and NMOS in series; NOR is the reverse. So whenever one network conducts, the other is open, and no current flows from VDD to GND at rest.

NAND and NOR come first

A single CMOS stage always inverts: a 1 on a gate can only turn on an NMOS, which pulls down. So NAND and NOR are the natural gates. AND and OR need an extra inverter: 2 transistors for NOT, 4 for NAND and NOR, 6 for AND and OR.

Where these appear next

The AND and OR gates in the multiplexer, decoder and adders on the next pages are built exactly this way: a NAND or NOR stage followed by an inverter, or folded into larger NAND-NAND logic by the chip designer.
