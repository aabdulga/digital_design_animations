2-to-4 Decoder

A decoder turns a binary number into a single active line. The two address inputs A1 A0 pick one of the four outputs D0 to D3 and drive it to 1; the other three stay at 0. Each AND gate recognises exactly one input combination, and the enable EN can switch all of them off at once.

Block view

What it does

Think of the address as a pointer. The 2-bit number A1 A0 points at one of the four output lines, and only that line goes to 1: the outputs are one-hot. With EN = 0 the pointer is switched off and every output stays 0.

One-hot outputs

When enabled, exactly one output is 1. Output Dk is the minterm mk of the address: D2 = EN·A1·A0'. A decoder is a minterm generator.

Why an enable?

With EN = 0 every output is 0. Two 2-to-4 decoders make a 3-to-8: feed A2' to one enable and A2 to the other, so only one half can ever light.

Any function

Every Boolean function is a sum of minterms, so a decoder plus one OR gate implements it. For F = m1 + m2, OR together D1 and D2 (that is XOR of A1 and A0).
