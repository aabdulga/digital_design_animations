State Machines

Traffic Light Controller (FSM)

A Moore finite state machine runs a simple intersection. Four states give the main street and the side street green in turn. Two timers, T30 and T5, are its only inputs, and its six outputs MR MY MG SR SY SG drive the lamps. Both streets get equal time: 30 s of green, then 5 s of yellow, so one full cycle takes 70 s.

The intersection

What it does

The controller hands the green to one street at a time. When a street's green has lasted 30 s it turns yellow for 5 s, then red, and the other street gets its turn. Cars stop at the stop line on red; on yellow, cars already close to the line keep going and the rest stop.

Speed

The walk buttons, sensors and walk lamp (drawn faint) are not inputs of this simple controller; it ignores them.

State machine

logic 0timer output at 1clk / reset / start at 1 Output lines at 1 take the colour of their lamp. Thick lines are 2-bit buses.

The clock ticks once per simulated second. The timers count on the falling clock edge, half a second before each rising edge, so T30 and T5 are already settled when the state register samples the next state. The slides give no state encoding; this page uses S1 = 00, S2 = 01, S3 = 10, S4 = 11. That is a choice: any four distinct 2-bit codes would work, with different logic inside the boxes.

History (last 80 s)

Each grid column is 10 s. T30 and T5 rise for the last half second of their state, and the state changes at the next rising edge. The small numbers above the state row mark each change in seconds.

A Moore machine

The six lamp outputs depend only on the current state, never directly on the timers. That is why they are written under each state in the diagram, and why the output logic box sees only the 2-bit state. The lamps change exactly at clock edges.

Timers as inputs

The FSM cannot count 30 seconds by itself with only four states, so a timer does the counting and reports a single bit: expired or not. Starting the timer on state entry is what makes "stay while T30 = 0" mean "stay for 30 s": every green and every yellow is measured from the moment it began.

A smarter controller

The sensors and walk buttons in the intersection could become extra inputs. For example, the main street could keep its green until a car waits on the side street sensor, and a walk request could add states that light the walk lamp. Each new input adds columns to the next-state table.
