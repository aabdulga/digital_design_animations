Shift Register

A 4-bit shift-right register. Four D flip-flops share one clock, and each flip-flop's D input is the previous flip-flop's Q. On every rising edge the whole word moves one position to the right: shr_in enters at Q3, and Q0 falls out as shr_out.

Block view

What it does

Think of a row of four seats. On every clock edge everybody moves one seat to the right: a new bit sits down at Q3 from shr_in, and the bit in Q0 leaves through shr_out.

Shifts the word in LSB first (rightmost bit first), one bit per cycle, so after 4 edges Q3 Q2 Q1 Q0 holds it in parallel: serial-to-parallel conversion. Four more edges push it out through shr_out: parallel-to-serial.

Timing diagram

A bit written on shr_in appears on Q3 after the next edge, then walks down one row per cycle: follow the diagonal.

One position per edge

All four flip-flops sample their D inputs at the same rising edge, so each one captures the value its neighbour held just before the edge. A bit therefore moves exactly one stage per clock, never further.

Serial in, parallel out

Feed a word one bit at a time into shr_in. After 4 edges all four bits are available at once on Q3..Q0. This is how a serial link (one wire) is turned back into a parallel word.

Parallel out, serial out

Keep clocking and the stored word leaves through shr_out, one bit per cycle. The same chain also works as a 4-cycle delay line: whatever enters at shr_in appears at shr_out four edges later.
