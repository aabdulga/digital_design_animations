4-bit Ripple-Carry Adder

Four full adders in a row add two 4-bit numbers, one column each, just like adding by hand. The carry out of each column is the carry in of the next one to its left. A column cannot finish until its carry has arrived, so the carry ripples from FA0 to FA3 and the sum is wrong for a moment. Change an input and watch it settle.

Carry propagation after the last change

τ is one stage delay (the slider). Every stage reacts to its own A and B bits within the first τ, but each carry needs one more τ per stage, so S can show wrong values until the last carry has arrived.

Delay grows with n

In a full adder the carry passes through two gate levels (AND, then OR). In the worst case it must cross every stage, so an n-bit ripple adder needs about 2n gate delays. 64 bits means about 128 gate delays, far too slow for a fast CPU.

Why carry-lookahead exists

Each stage either generates a carry (G = AB) or propagates one (P = A⊕B). A lookahead unit computes C2 = G1 + P1G0 + P1P0C0 and so on straight from G, P and C0 in two levels, so no carry has to wait for its neighbour.

Signed overflow

For two's-complement numbers, C4 is not an error flag. Overflow happens when the carry into the sign bit differs from the carry out of it: V = C4 ⊕ C3. Example: 0111 + 0001 = 1000, so 7 + 1 gives −8 and V = 1.
